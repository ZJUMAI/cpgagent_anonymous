# TCGA-J2-8194 Full Patient Report

## Pathology-Molecular Integration Summary

This 69-year-old female with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) demonstrates a molecular profile characterized by a KRAS mutation (Gly12Cys) with concurrent STK11 and KEAP1 mutations, a combination associated with poor prognosis and potential resistance to immunotherapy/chemotherapy. The pathology report confirms a KRAS mutation and wild-type EGFR, consistent with the molecular findings. No activating mutations or high amplifications were detected in other common driver genes (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, RET, NTRK, etc.). EGFR shows copy gain (CNV=1), but this is not equivalent to an EGFR activating mutation and does not independently imply EGFR-TKI sensitivity. Copy number variants are predominantly widespread copy gains (1226 genes), with no high amplification or deep deletion events. The tumor is a 4 cm adenocarcinoma with papillary/bronchoalveolar (lepidic) pattern, grade 1/4, pT3 pN0 pMX, with invasion through visceral pleura into superficial diaphragmatic soft tissue.

## Molecular Report

### Key Lung Cancer Molecular Interpretation

This case is a 69-year-old female with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). The molecular profile is characterized primarily by a KRAS mutation combined with STK11/KEAP1 co-mutations. No activating mutations or high amplifications were detected in common driver genes (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, RET, NTRK, etc.), presenting a "common-driver-negative / CNV-dominant" pattern. However, CNVs are predominantly copy gains, with no high amplification or deep deletion events.

- **Core driver/treatment-related genes**: A KRAS mutation is present, a common driver event in LUAD, but no activating mutations were found in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET, ERBB2, RET, NTRK1/2/3. EGFR shows copy gain (CNV=1), but note: EGFR copy gain is not equivalent to an EGFR activating mutation and cannot independently infer EGFR-TKI sensitivity. NTRK1 also shows copy gain, but without evidence of fusion or mutation, its clinical significance is limited.
- **Tumor suppressor/prognosis/drug resistance-related genes**: Both STK11 and KEAP1 harbor mutations. Co-mutation of these genes in LUAD often indicates poor prognosis and potential resistance to immunotherapy/chemotherapy. No mutations or significant CNV abnormalities were observed in TP53, SMARCA4, RB1, CDKN2A, NF1, ATM, etc.
- **Significant molecular abnormalities in this case**: CNVs are predominantly widespread copy gains (1226 genes), with no high amplification or deep deletion genes. The RNA-seq Top 20 highly expressed genes (e.g., PHOX2B, FAM65B, etc.) do not directly correspond to key driver genes or CNV events and are therefore not used as primary interpretation evidence. Overall CNV burden is high, but lacks targetable high amplification events.
